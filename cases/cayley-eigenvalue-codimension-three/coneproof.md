# Closed monotone orbit cones for three- and four-cycles

This supplement supplies the threshold estimates and exact fixed-order inputs
for the classifications below. [checkcone.py](checkcone.py) reconstructs the
symbolic gaps and the complete fixed $S_6$ boundary over the rationals.
General order is handled by the analytic bounds and stable-pattern proof.

## 1. Definitions, statements, and inputs

Let $R\subseteq[n]$, $C=R^c$, and assign each $k$-cycle with support
$A$ the weight $f(|A\cap R|)$, where $f$ is nonnegative and
nondecreasing on its feasible intersection sizes. Write $T_f$ for the
inverse-closed group-algebra sum. We work in unitary representations.

Let $K_Y^{(k)}$ be the sum of all $k$-cycles supported in $Y$;
superscripts are omitted when the length is fixed. Put
```math
B_s=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\text{ a }k\text{-cycle}\\
|\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)\cap R|\ge s}}\sigma,\qquad B_0=K_n.
\tag{1}
```
The degree blocks are $(n)$ for even $k$, and $(n),(1^n)$ for odd
$k$. The target blocks are the standard block $(n-1,1)$, together
with its sign twist for odd $k$. “Standard-only” refers to which
irreducible blocks attain the largest nondegree eigenvalue; it does not
assert uniform multiplicity inside the standard block.

**Theorem.**

1. For $k=3,n\ge5,1\le r=|R|\le n-2$, write
   ```math
   T_f=aK_n+uB_1+vB_2+wK_R,\qquad a,u,v,w\ge0.
   \tag{2}
   ```
   Omit thresholds having no feasible support. If $a+u+v>0$, exactly
   the standard block and its sign twist attain the largest nondegree
   value. The excluded nonzero ray is the pure local sum $wK_R$.
   In particular, for $r=1,2$, every nonzero closed monotone weight
   has the strict classification.
2. For $k=4,r=2,n\ge6$, every nonzero closed monotone weight is
   standard-only when $n\ge7$. At $n=6$, every nonconstant such
   weight is standard-only. On the positive constant ray,
   $(2,2,2)$ is the unique additional attaining block.
3. For $k=4,r=3,n\ge6$, every nonzero closed monotone weight is
   standard-only when $n\ge7$. At $n=6$, the unique additional
   attaining block is $(2,2,2)$, occurring exactly when
   $f(1)=f(2)=f(3)>0$. The value $f(0)$ is infeasible at this order.

There is no claim for four marked points, larger marked sets in the
four-cycle problem, or general $k\ge4$.

We use these inputs with explicit ranges.

- The [complete proof of Conjecture 4.7](https://github.com/randomcat4/euler-combinatorics-conjecture-cases/blob/main/cases/cayley-eigenvalue-codimension-three/paper/full_conjecture.pdf)
  gives strict uniqueness for
  ```math
  C(N,k;E)^+=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\text{ a }k\text{-cycle in }S_N\\
  E\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)}}\sigma
  ```
  when $N\ge5,\ 1\le |E|<k\le N-2$. Its standard top space contains
  vectors vanishing on $E$ and summing to zero on $E^c$. The values
  needed here are
  ```math
  \mu_2(N,3;2)=2(N-3),\quad
  \mu_2(N,4;2)=3(N-3)(N-4)-2,\quad
  \mu_2(N,4;3)=6(N-4).
  \tag{3}
  ```
  The original question and non-strict bounds appear in
  Li--Xia--Zhou, [*The second largest eigenvalue of some nonnormal
  Cayley graphs on symmetric groups*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02427),
  Theorems 1.2, 1.3 and Conjecture 4.7. Standard values also appear in
  Siemons--Zalesski, [*On the second largest eigenvalue of some Cayley
  graphs of the symmetric group*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10801-021-01080-4).
- For $n\ge7$, the normal four-cycle class is strictly standard-only
  by Li--Xia--Zhou, [*The second largest eigenvalue of normal Cayley
  graphs on symmetric groups generated by cycles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04022),
  Theorem 3.5(d), with $I=\{4\}$.
- We use the standard branching rule and the Jucys--Murphy identities
  for the three- and four-cycle central scalars:
  ```math
  \kappa_s(\lambda)=\sum_{b\in\lambda}c(b)^2-\binom s2,\qquad
  \zeta_s(\lambda)=\sum_{b\in\lambda}c(b)^3-(2s-3)\sum_{b\in\lambda}c(b),
  \tag{4}
  ```
  where $c(i,j)=j-i$.

## 2. Threshold combination

Monotonicity gives
```math
T_f=f(0)K_n+\sum_{s\ge1}(f(s)-f(s-1))B_s.
\tag{5}
```
If intersection zero is infeasible, take the harmless extension
$f(0)=f(1)$. The common standard comparison space is
```math
W=\{x\in\mathbb C^n:x|_R=0,\ \sum_{i\in C}x_i=0\},
\qquad \dim W=|C|-1>0.
\tag{6}
```
Each full threshold ray below has standard maximum $\alpha_i$ attained
on $W$. Thus a nonnegative combination has standard maximum
$\sum_i c_i\alpha_i$, and in every nontarget block Weyl's inequality gives
```math
\sum_i c_i\alpha_i-\lambda_{\max}\Bigl(\sum_i c_iA_i\Bigr)
\ge\sum_i c_i\bigl(\alpha_i-\lambda_{\max}(A_i)\bigr).
\tag{7}
```
A positive coefficient on one strict ray suffices. Commutativity is
unnecessary. The embedded auxiliary operators in Section 7 are not
individually asserted to have their standard maxima on $W$.

## 3. Three-cycle hit ray

Put $q=|C|\ge2$ and relabel $C=[q]$. Then
```math
B_1=K_n-K_q
=\sum_{i=q+1}^nJ_i^2-\Bigl(\binom n2-\binom q2\Bigr)I.
\tag{8}
```
For any legal tableau deletion from a nonrow, noncolumn shape to size
$q$, the deleted contents satisfy
```math
\sum_{s=q+1}^n d_s^2\le\sum_{s=q+1}^n(s-2)^2.
\tag{9}
```
While the current $s$-box diagram is neither a row nor a column,
every removable corner has absolute content at most $s-2$. If the
path never enters a row or column, summing proves (9), and equality
at the first step requires the standard or conjugate-standard shape.

If deletion first enters a row of size $t$, the preceding shape is
$(t,1)$. The actual final square sum is
$1+\sum_{j=q}^{t-1}j^2$, whereas the comparison is
$\sum_{j=q-1}^{t-1}j^2$; their difference is
$1-(q-1)^2\le0$. The column case is identical. Equality in the
preceding steps again forces the initial standard or conjugate-standard
shape. Consequently
```math
\beta_{\rm hit}=\sum_{s=q+1}^n(s-2)^2-\binom n2+\binom q2
\tag{10}
```
is attained only by the target blocks. Deleting first-row endpoints
from $(n-1,1)$ to $(q-1,1)$ realizes it on $W$.
When $q=2$, $B_1=K_n$, and the whole standard block is a top
space; the representation-level classification remains unchanged.

Taking $q=2$ also proves strictness for the normal three-cycle class.
The deletion estimate gives the usual content-square bound for
nonrow, noncolumn $\alpha\vdash r$:
```math
\kappa_r(\alpha)\le A_r:=\kappa_r(r-1,1)
=\frac{r(r-2)(r-4)}3.
\tag{11}
```
For $r\ge5$, the first two squares sum to one and the remaining
squares are bounded by $\sum_{s=3}^r(s-2)^2$; subtracting
$\binom r2$ gives (11). For $r=3,4$, direct content calculation
gives the same bounds $-1,0$.

## 4. Three-cycle majority ray

Write $M_R=B_2$. Its degree and value on $W$ are
```math
D_M=2\Bigl[\binom r2q+\binom r3\Bigr],\qquad
\beta_M=D_M-r(r-1)=r(r-1)\Bigl[q+\frac{r-5}3\Bigr].
\tag{12}
```
Indeed the natural Laplacian has cross-edge conductance $r-1$
and no edges within $C$, so its value on $W$ is $r(r-1)$.
For $r=1$, $M_R=0$; for $r=2$, it is the global hard-containment
ray in (3). Assume $r\ge3$.

For $H_E=C(n,3;E)^+$, support counting gives
```math
M_R=\sum_{\substack{E\subseteq R\\|E|=2}}H_E-2K_R
=\sum_{c\in C}K_{R\cup\{c\}}-(q-1)K_R.
\tag{13}
```
The first sum counts an all-marked triple three times; the second
counts it $q$ times. The corrections give the identities.

Since $M_R$ commutes with $S_R$, decompose a nondegree parent
block into its $S_R$-isotypic parts. On the trivial or sign type,
$K_R=2\binom r3 I$, and the first identity gives
```math
\lambda_{\max}(M_R)\le\binom r2\,2(n-3)-4\binom r3=\beta_M.
\tag{14}
```
For a nontarget parent every $H_E$ has a strict global gap by
complete C4.7, making (14) strict. The individual $H_E$ need not
preserve the isotypic part: only their full-parent Rayleigh bounds
are used.

On any other $S_R$ type $\alpha$, one-box branching gives the
local scalars $\kappa_r(\alpha)+d^2-r$. Every addable content
has $|d|\le r-1$. The second identity and (11) therefore give
```math
\lambda_{\max}(M_R|\alpha)\le A_r+q((r-1)^2-r),\qquad
\beta_M-A_r-q((r-1)^2-r)=q(2r-1)-r>0.
\tag{15}
```
These bounds cover every isotypic part, including those of the
standard parent, so the attained value (12) is its maximum.

The local ray $K_R$ has the degree bound $2\binom r3$ in every
representation, attained on $W$, which it fixes pointwise. The
normal, hit, and majority rays are strict; (7) proves the first
claim of the theorem. The pure local ray is confined to the subgroup
on $R$ and does not have the asserted global uniqueness.

## 5. The two-marked four-cycle hit ray

Put $m=n-2$. For a branch $\nu\subseteq\lambda$ with two
added-box contents $r,s$, formula (4) gives
```math
h(\lambda,\nu)=g_m(r)+g_m(s)-4C(\nu),\quad
g_m(x)=x^3-(2m+1)x,\quad C(\nu)=\sum c(b).
\tag{16}
```
The common standard branch
$(\lambda,\nu)=((m+1,1),(m-1,1))$ has contents $m-1,m$ and value
```math
H_m=m(m-3)(2m-3).
\tag{17}
```
The two Pieri rules account for all $S_2\times S_m$ branches.
Every two-box skew shape is horizontal or vertical, or both, so it
suffices to bound every containment pair.

For $m\ge5$, write $\nu_1=m-\rho$, with tail $\tau\vdash\rho$.
Then
```math
C(\nu)=\binom{m-\rho}2+C(\tau)-\rho
\ge\frac{m(m-2\rho-1)}2.
\tag{18}
```
Every new content satisfies $-(\rho+2)\le x\le m-\rho+1$.
On the positive half-axis the maximum of $g_m$ is bounded by
$\max(0,g_m(m-\rho+1))$; on the negative half-axis its maximum is
$M_m=2(2m+1)^{3/2}/(3\sqrt3)>0$. Thus
```math
h\le\max(U_+,U_-),\quad
U_+=2g_m(m-\rho+1)+2m(2\rho+1-m),\quad
U_-=2M_m+2m(2\rho+1-m).
\tag{19}
```
For $2\le\rho\le m-1$, the difference
```math
F_+=H_m-U_+
=6m^2\rho-9m^2-6m\rho^2+4m\rho+7m+2\rho^3-6\rho^2+4\rho
\tag{20}
```
is strictly concave in $\rho$, since $F_+''=12(\rho-m-1)<0$.
Its endpoint values are $3m(m-3)>0$ and
$2m^3-11m^2+19m-12=2z^3+19z^2+59z+58>0$, with $m=z+5$.

The negative bound is worst at $\rho=m-1$. Both sides of
$2M_m<m(2m^2-11m+11)$ are positive for $m\ge5$. After squaring,
its difference, with $m=z+5$, is
```math
27m^2(2m^2-11m+11)^2-16(2m+1)^3
=108z^6+2052z^5+15255z^4+55438z^3+98895z^2+71004z+3004>0.
\tag{21}
```
All $\rho\ge2$ branches are therefore strict. The other placements
are exhausted by these tables:

| $\rho=0$, contents | $H_m-h$ |
|---|---|
| $(m,-1)$ | $m(m-3)(m-2)$ |
| $(-1,0)$ | $m(m-1)(2m-5)$ |
| $(-1,-2)$ | $(m-3)(2m^2-m-2)$ |

The omitted first-row double addition is the degree branch.

| $\rho=1$, contents | $H_m-h$ |
|---|---|
| $(m-1,m)$ | $0$ |
| $(m-1,0)$ | $m(m^2-2m-1)$ |
| $(m-1,-2)$ | $(m-3)(m-1)(m+2)$ |
| $(0,1)$ | $m(m-1)(2m-5)$ |
| $(0,-2)$ | $(m-1)(2m^2-5m-6)$ |
| $(-2,-3)$ | $(m-3)(m+2)(2m-5)$ |

The row-increment patterns are
$(2),(1,1),(1,0,1),(0,2),(0,1,1),(0,0,1,1)$, so the latter
list is complete. Every nonzero entry is positive for $m\ge5$.
The only zero is the common standard branch, proving strictness and
the standard maximum.

For the full two-marked cone the ray values on $W$ are
```math
K_n:\frac{n(n-2)(n-3)(n-5)}4,\quad
B_1:m(m-3)(2m-3),\quad B_2:3(n-3)(n-4)-2.
\tag{22}
```
The normal ray is strict for $n\ge7$, the hit ray is strict by this
section, and the global hard-containment ray is strict by C4.7.
All attain their standard maxima on $W$; (7) proves every nonzero
closed combination for $n\ge7$.

## 6. The three-marked four-cycle hit ray

Put $m=n-3\ge4$. With three added-box contents, the scalar of
$B_1=K_{m+3}-K_m$ is
```math
h=\sum_{i=1}^3[x_i^3-(2m+3)x_i]-6C(\nu).
\tag{23}
```
The branch $(\lambda,\nu)=((m+2,1),(m-1,1))$ has contents
$m-1,m,m+1$, giving $H_m=3m(m-1)(m-2)$.

For $m\ge5$, use (18) and
$-(\rho+3)\le x\le m-\rho+2$. Put
$g(x)=x^3-(2m+3)x$ and $M_m=2(2m+3)^{3/2}/(3\sqrt3)$.
Then
```math
h\le\max(U_+,U_-),\quad
U_+=3g(m-\rho+2)+3m(2\rho+1-m),\quad
U_-=3M_m+3m(2\rho+1-m).
\tag{24}
```
The positive difference is
```math
H_m-U_+=3(\rho-2)
[3m^2-3m\rho+2m+\rho^2-4\rho+1].
\tag{25}
```
The bracket decreases for $3\le\rho\le m-1$; its value at $m-1$
is $m^2-m+6>0$. Thus $U_+<H_m$ for $\rho\ge3$.
The negative bound is worst at $\rho=m-1$; the required
$M_m<m(m-1)(m-3)$ has positive sides. Its squared difference is
```math
27m^2(m-1)^2(m-3)^2-4(2m+3)^3
=27z^6+594z^5+5319z^4+24700z^3+62124z^2+78024z+34412>0,
\tag{26}
```
where $m=z+5$. Hence every $\rho\ge3$ branch is strict.
The $\rho=2$ case has zero slack in (25) and is included in the
exact low-tail argument below.

### Infinite stable-pattern coverage

For $\rho=0,1,2$, the tails are exactly
$()),(1),(2),(1,1)$. A branch is specified by the tail and row
increments $\delta_i=\lambda_i-\nu_i$, with sum three.
For $m\ge8$, the original first row has length at least six,
whereas every lower row in $\lambda$ has length at most $2+3=5$.
Changing both first rows by the same amount, down to $m=8$ or
up from it, preserves the partition inequalities, containment, and
increments. Therefore enumeration at $m=8$ exhausts precisely
the patterns for every $m\ge8$.

Each first-row added content is $m$ plus a fixed integer; each
lower content is constant, and
```math
C(\nu)=\frac{(m-\rho)(m-\rho-1)}2+C(\tau)-\rho.
\tag{27}
```
Substitution into (23) gives an exact polynomial gap of degree at
most three. The checker constructs all 44 patterns from actual
containment pairs at $m=8$ and performs this symbolic substitution.
Excluding the one degree pattern and the one common standard
pattern, every coefficient of the gap at $m=z+5$ is nonnegative,
with positive constant term. The standard polynomial is zero.

At $m=5,6,7$, every actual low-tail pair is evaluated directly;
the pair counts are $41,43,44$. At $m=4$, where the coarse
negative estimate does not suffice, all 42 containment pairs are
evaluated, including every tail size. None gives an additional
nondegree zero or negative gap.

This is a finite symbolic reduction with a proved infinite coverage
step, not extrapolation from a bounded order scan. Combined with
(24)--(26), it proves the hit ray strict for every $m\ge4$.
Its standard maximum is attained on $W$.

## 7. The middle threshold and embedded degree exclusion

Let $m=n-3\ge4$. For $S\subseteq R$ of size two, let $t_S$
be the other marked point and define
```math
D_S=\sum_{\substack{\sigma\text{ a four-cycle}\\
S\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(\sigma),\
t_S\notin\operatorname{supp}(\sigma)}}\sigma.
```
It is $C(m+2,4;S)^+$ in the subgroup on $C\cup S$, of order
$(n-1)!$. Supports with exactly two marked points occur in exactly
one $D_S$; supports with three marked points occur in $B_3$.
Thus
```math
B_2=\sum_{|S|=2,\ S\subseteq R}D_S+B_3.
\tag{28}
```

A parent other than $(n)$ and $(n-1,1)$ has no trivial
$S_{n-1}$-child: adding one box to $(n-1)$ yields only those
two parents. The bound therefore applies on every child, giving
```math
\lambda_{\max}(D_S)\le d_m=3(m-1)(m-2)-2.
\tag{29}
```
The range is legal, since $m+2\ge6$ and $4\le(m+2)-2$.
Four-cycles have no sign degree exception. On the same nontarget
parent the global ray satisfies
$\lambda_{\max}(B_3)<e_m=6(m-1)$ by C4.7. Hence
```math
\lambda_{\max}(B_2)<3d_m+e_m
=3(3m-1)(m-2)=:J_m.
\tag{30}
```

On the entire standard parent an individual $D_S$ can have a
subgroup-trivial branch with its degree value; (29) is not claimed
there. Compute the standard maximum of the combined $B_2$
directly instead. Its degree is $D=3m(3m-1)$, and its three
nontrivial natural branches have scalars
```math
W:J_m,\qquad
\text{zero sum on }R:2m(m-2),\qquad
\text{contrast line}:(5m-3)(m-2).
\tag{31}
```
A point in $C$ belongs to $3m-2$ supports, and a pair in $C$
belongs to three; diagonal minus off-diagonal is
$D-6(3m-2)-6=J_m$.
A point in $R$ belongs to $m^2$ supports, and a pair in $R$
has off-diagonal coefficient $m(m+1)$, giving the second scalar.
A cross-pair has coefficient $4m-2$, giving
$D-(m+3)(4m-2)$ on the contrast line.
The gaps from the last two scalars are
$(7m-3)(m-2)>0$ and $4m(m-2)>0$.

Thus $J_m$ is the standard maximum on $W$. On $W$ each
$D_S$ acts by $d_m$, and $B_3$ acts by $e_m$, matching
(30). The normal, hit, middle, and hard-containment rays are now
all strict with common standard attainment. This proves the
three-marked nonzero closed cone for $n\ge7$.

## 8. The exact $n=6$ boundaries

The embedded argument is not used at $n=6$: its child would be
$C(5,4;2)^+$, outside $k\le N-2$. The checker reconstructs
the following fixed-group inputs exactly.

| Marked size | Effective rays | Standard values | Extra normal equality |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2$ | $K_6,B_1,B_2$ | $18,20,16$ | only $(2,2,2)$ |
| $3$ | $K_6,B_2,B_3$ | $18,24,12$ | only $(2,2,2)$ |

Every nontarget block is strictly below the displayed values on
the two noncentral rays; all standard values are attained on $W$.
For two marked points, put
$u=f(0),v=f(1)-f(0),w=f(2)-f(1)$.
The target is $18u+20v+16w$. If $v+w>0$, (7) is strict
even with $u=0$. If $v=w=0,u>0$, exactly the extra block
$(2,2,2)$ ties.

For three marked points, intersection zero is impossible and
$B_1=K_6$. Put
$u=f(1),v=f(2)-f(1),w=f(3)-f(2)$.
The target is $18u+24v+12w$. The same reasoning gives
strictness for $v+w>0$, including $u=0$, and precisely the
extra $(2,2,2)$ tie when $f(1)=f(2)=f(3)>0$.
These fixed-group inequalities prove each complete continuous
parameter cone at order six.

### Exact reconstruction

The script enumerates all 11 partitions of six and all standard
tableaux. In a rational seminormal basis, with
$d_T=c_T(i+1)-c_T(i)$, it uses
```math
\rho((i,i+1))e_T=\frac1{d_T}e_T+
\left(1+\frac1{d_T}\right)e_{(i,i+1)T},
\tag{32}
```
omitting the second term when the swapped tableau is not standard.
It verifies the Coxeter relations and every diagonal
Jucys--Murphy content value exactly. Tableau counts agree with the
hook formula; squared dimensions sum to $720$.

All 90 four-cycles are generated and their matrices summed by
support intersection, with counts $(6,48,36)$ for two marked
points and $(0,18,54,18)$ for three. The normal matrices agree
with $\sum c^3-9\sum c$.
For each ray the characteristic polynomial is computed over
$\mathbb Q$; its exact target factor is removed, and a Sturm
count excludes all remaining roots above the target. No
nontarget noncentral target factor occurs. The only nontarget
normal target factor is in $(2,2,2)$.
Standard target multiplicities are $(5,3,3)$ and $(5,2,2)$,
respectively. Natural permutation matrices are independently
checked to have the displayed action on $W$.

With Python and SymPy, run:

~~~text
python checkcone.py
python checkcone.py --full
~~~

The second command prints all 44 exact symbolic gap polynomials
and shifted coefficients. Neither command reads stored
certificates or pass/fail flags. The fixed-order computation
certifies only the $S_6$ inputs; the general result uses the
analytic bounds and the infinite coverage argument above.
