\title{A Symbolic Infinitesimal Flex for the Dimension-Seven Slice of a Volume-Rigidity Conjecture}
\author{Prepared with the EULER system}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Cruickshank, Jackson, and Tanigawa conjectured that if \(\cS\) is a
simplicial \(2\)-sphere on \(\lceil d/2\rceil+2\) vertices and \(Z\)
is a disjoint set of \(d-3\) vertices, then the hypergraph of
\((d-2)\)-faces of the simplicial cone \(\cS*Z\) is volume rigid in
\(\R^d\).  We give a symbolic counterexample to the complete \(d=7\)
slice.  In fact, the same construction applies to every simplicial
\(2\)-sphere on six vertices.  For a generic placement, form the
\(7\times 3\) matrix \(D\) of the three differences among the four cone
vertices.  We construct a nonzero symmetric matrix \(B=DKD^{\mathsf T}\)
and the velocity field
\(u(v)=B(p(v)-p(z_0))\).  Two trace identities, one for hyperedges of
the form \(e\cup Z\) and one for hyperedges of the form
\(t\cup(Z\setminus\{z\})\), show symbolically that all \(44\) squared
five-volume derivatives vanish.  The motion is not Euclidean-trivial.
Thus the generic Jacobian rank is at most \(41\), below the rigidity
target \(42\).  Finite enumeration and exact computations are reported
only as independent cross-checks of the combinatorics and the symbolic
certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cruickshank, Jackson, and Tanigawa introduced a framework for volume
rigidity of uniform hypergraphs and simplicial complexes
\cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026}.  Among their proposed base cases
for higher-dimensional results is the following statement
\cite[Conjecture~22]{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026}: for \(d\geq 4\),
if \(\cS\) is a simplicial \(2\)-sphere on
\(\lceil d/2\rceil+2\) vertices and \(Z\) is a disjoint set of
\(d-3\) vertices, then the hypergraph \(H_{d-2}(\cS*Z)\) is volume
rigid in \(\R^d\).

This note concerns exactly the slice \(d=7\).  Here \(\cS\) has six
vertices, \(Z\) has four vertices, and \(H_5(\cS*Z)\) has ten vertices
and \(44\) hyperedges.  A generic placement has \(70\) scalar velocity
coordinates.  The space of translations and infinitesimal rotations
has dimension
\[
  7+\binom{7}{2}=28,
\]
so infinitesimal volume rigidity would require rank \(70-28=42\).

Our main result is a symbolic rank obstruction that is uniform over
all six-vertex simplicial \(2\)-spheres.  It does not rely on a special
placement, a finite-field rank computation, or the finite enumeration
of the two combinatorial types.  Those computations are included only
as reproducibility checks.  The result does not address \(d\geq 8\),
the broader manifold conjecture in \cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026},
nongeneric placements, or global uniqueness.

\section{Definitions and the dimension-seven slice}
\label{sec:definitions}

\subsection{Simplicial cones and their hypergraphs}

For a simplicial complex \(\cS\) and a finite set \(Z\) disjoint from
\(V(\cS)\), define
\[
  \cS*Z=\{X\cup Z': X\in\cS,\ Z'\subseteq Z\}.
\]
For a simplicial complex \(\mathcal K\), the hypergraph \(H_k(\mathcal
K)\) has vertex set \(V(\mathcal K)\) and one hyperedge for every
\(k\)-face of \(\mathcal K\); hence every hyperedge has \(k+1\)
vertices.

Fix a simplicial \(2\)-sphere \(\cS\) on vertices
\(0,1,\ldots,5\), and let
\[
  Z=\{6,7,8,9\}=\{z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3\},
  \qquad z_i=6+i.
\]
The six-element hyperedges of \(H_5(\cS*Z)\) are exactly
\begin{equation}
  e\cup Z \quad (e\in\cS^{(1)}),
  \qquad
  t\cup\bigl(Z\setminus\{z_i\}\bigr)
  \quad (t\in\cS^{(2)},\ 0\leq i\leq 3).
  \label{eq:hyperedge-families}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a six-element face of the join contains either two sphere
vertices and all four cone vertices, or three sphere vertices and
three cone vertices.

Every triangulated \(2\)-sphere on six vertices has twelve edges and
eight triangles: Euler's relation and double counting edge--triangle
incidences give
\[
  6-f_1+f_2=2,
  \qquad 2f_1=3f_2,
\]
and hence \((f_1,f_2)=(12,8)\).  Thus
\[
  |E(H_5(\cS*Z))|=12+4\cdot 8=44.
\]

\subsection{The two six-vertex sphere types}
\label{sec:types}

For completeness, the two combinatorial types of six-vertex
simplicial \(2\)-sphere may be represented by the following triangle
sets:
\begin{align*}
  T_{\mathrm{st}}
  &={\{}012,013,023,124,134,235,245,345{\}},\\
  T_{\mathrm{oct}}
  &={\{}012,013,024,034,125,135,245,345{\}}.
\end{align*}
Their edge sets are, respectively,
\begin{align*}
  E_{\mathrm{st}}
  &={\{}01,02,03,12,13,14,23,24,25,34,35,45{\}},\\
  E_{\mathrm{oct}}
  &={\{}01,02,03,04,12,13,15,24,25,34,35,45{\}}.
\end{align*}
The complete ordered lists of all \(44\) hyperedges for each type are
given in \cref{app:hyperedges}.  The symbolic argument below only uses
the two forms in \eqref{eq:hyperedge-families}, so it applies uniformly
and does not depend on the classification.

\subsection{Squared-volume Jacobian}

Let \(p:V(H_5(\cS*Z))\to\R^7\) be a placement.  It is \emph{generic}
if its \(70\) scalar coordinates are algebraically independent over
\(\Q\).  For a hyperedge
\(\Delta=\{v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_5\}\), let \(M_\Delta(p)\) be the
\(5\times7\) matrix whose \(i\)-th row is
\((p(v_i)-p(v_0))^{\mathsf T}\), and put
\[
  g_\Delta(p)=\det\bigl(M_\Delta(p)M_\Delta(p)^{\mathsf T}\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
  g_\Delta(p)=(5!)^2\Vol_5(p(\Delta))^2.
\]
Let \(J_{\cS}(p)\) be the \(44\times70\) Jacobian with one row
\(\nabla g_\Delta(p)\) for each hyperedge \(\Delta\).  At a generic
placement this Jacobian has the same rank and right kernel as the
volume-rigidity matrix of \cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026}: the
Gram determinant differs from squared five-volume by the fixed nonzero
factor \((5!)^2\), and the additional row scalings used in that paper
are nonzero at a generic placement.

The Euclidean-trivial infinitesimal motions are
\[
  u(v)=A p(v)+t,
  \qquad A^{\mathsf T}=-A,\quad t\in\R^7.
\]
A generic ten-point placement affinely spans \(\R^7\), so these
motions form a \(28\)-dimensional subspace of \(\ker J_{\cS}(p)\).
Consequently the \(d=7\) specialization of Conjecture~22 predicts
\(\rank J_{\cS}(p)=42\).

\section{Main result}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:main}
Let \(\cS\) be any simplicial \(2\)-sphere on six vertices, let \(Z\)
be a disjoint four-element set, and let \(p:V(H_5(\cS*Z))\to\R^7\)
be generic.  Then
\[
  \rank J_{\cS}(p)\leq 41.
\]
In particular, \(H_5(\cS*Z)\) is not volume rigid in \(\R^7\), and
the \(d=7\) slice of Conjecture~22 in
\cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026} is false.
\end{theorem}

The remainder of this section constructs a single additional symbolic
infinitesimal motion.  The equivalence between generic volume rigidity
and generic infinitesimal volume rigidity is Proposition~6 of
\cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026}.

\subsection{A trace formula for squared volume}

For a linear subspace \(U\subseteq\R^7\), write \(P_U\) for its
orthogonal projector.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:trace}
Let \(\Delta\) be a nondegenerate \(5\)-simplex with direction space
\(U\), and let \(B\) be a symmetric \(7\times7\) matrix.  Under the
affine velocity field \(u(q)=B(q-q_0)\), where \(q_0\) is fixed,
\[
  \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}
  g_\Delta\bigl(q_0+(I+tB)(p-q_0)\bigr)
  =2g_\Delta(p)\tr(P_U B).
  \label{eq:trace-derivative}
\]
The same formula holds with \(g_\Delta\) replaced by squared
five-volume.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Translations do not affect edge differences.  If \(M=M_\Delta(p)\),
then the transformed difference matrix is
\(M_t=M(I+tB)^{\mathsf T}\).  Since \(B=B^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
  \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}M_tM_t^{\mathsf T}
  =2MBM^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Jacobi's determinant identity gives
\begin{align*}
  \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\det(M_tM_t^{\mathsf T})
  &=2\det(MM^{\mathsf T})
    \tr\!\left((MM^{\mathsf T})^{-1}MBM^{\mathsf T}\right)\\
  &=2g_\Delta(p)
    \tr\!\left(M^{\mathsf T}(MM^{\mathsf T})^{-1}M B\right).
\end{align*}
The matrix \(M^{\mathsf T}(MM^{\mathsf T})^{-1}M\) is \(P_U\),
which proves \eqref{eq:trace-derivative}.  Division by \((5!)^2\)
gives the squared-volume version.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The symbolic motion}

Translate the notation so that \(p(z_0)\) is the origin, and define
\[
  D=\begin{bmatrix}d_1&d_2&d_3\end{bmatrix}
   =\begin{bmatrix}
      p(z_1)-p(z_0)&p(z_2)-p(z_0)&p(z_3)-p(z_0)
    \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad G=D^{\mathsf T}D.
\]
Write
\[
  a=G_{12},\qquad b=G_{13},\qquad c=G_{23},
\]
and set
\[
  \xi=c-b,\qquad \eta=a-c,\qquad \zeta=b-a,
  \qquad
  K=\begin{pmatrix}
      0&\xi&\eta\\
      \xi&0&\zeta\\
      \eta&\zeta&0
    \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Finally, put
\begin{equation}
  B=DKD^{\mathsf T},
  \qquad
  u(v)=B\bigl(p(v)-p(z_0)\bigr).
  \label{eq:flex}
\end{equation}
This is a polynomial velocity vector in the placement coordinates.

The definitions immediately give
\begin{equation}
  \xi+\eta+\zeta=0,
  \qquad
  a\xi+b\eta+c\zeta=0.
  \label{eq:two-identities}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \tr(KG)=0,
  \qquad
  h^{\mathsf T}Kh=0
  \quad\text{for }h\in
  \{e_1,e_2,e_3,(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\}.
  \label{eq:facet-identities}
\end{equation}
The first equation follows because
\(\tr(KG)=2(a\xi+b\eta+c\zeta)\).  The three coordinate-vector
equations in the second statement are the zero diagonal of \(K\), and
the remaining equation is
\(2(\xi+\eta+\zeta)=0\).

The four covectors in \eqref{eq:facet-identities} are normal covectors
to the four facets of the tetrahedron with coordinates
\(0,e_1,e_2,e_3\) in the basis \(D\).  For a generic placement,
\(D\) has full column rank and \(a,b,c\) are not all equal.  The latter
equalities define a proper algebraic subset.  Hence \(K\neq0\), and
full column rank of \(D\) then implies \(B=DKD^{\mathsf T}\neq0\).
The matrix \(B\) is symmetric.

\subsection{Stationarity of all hyperedge volumes}

Let \(L=\im D\).  The operator \(B\) is supported on \(L\): it kills
\(L^\perp\), maps into \(L\), and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \tr(B)=\tr(DKD^{\mathsf T})=\tr(KG)=0.
  \label{eq:trace-B}
\end{equation}

First consider a hyperedge \(\Delta=e\cup Z\).  Its direction space
has the orthogonal decomposition
\[
  U_\Delta=L\oplus R,
\]
where \(R\subseteq L^\perp\) is spanned by the projections of the two
sphere vertices in \(e\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \tr(P_{U_\Delta}B)=\tr(P_LB)+\tr(P_RB)
  =\tr(B)=0.
  \label{eq:edge-trace}
\end{equation}

Now let
\(\Delta=t\cup(Z\setminus\{z_\ell\})\).  Denote by \(L_\ell\) the
two-dimensional direction space of the corresponding facet of the
tetrahedron on \(Z\).  Let \(h_\ell\) be its normal covector in
\(D\)-coordinates; thus
\[
  \{h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3\}
  =\{(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},e_1,e_2,e_3\}.
\]
The vector
\[
  n_\ell=DG^{-1}h_\ell
\]
is normal to \(L_\ell\) inside \(L\), because
\(D^{\mathsf T}n_\ell=h_\ell\).  From
\eqref{eq:facet-identities},
\begin{equation}
  n_\ell^{\mathsf T}Bn_\ell
  =h_\ell^{\mathsf T}Kh_\ell=0.
  \label{eq:normal-quadratic}
\end{equation}
Since \(L=L_\ell\oplus\operatorname{span}(n_\ell)\) orthogonally,
\eqref{eq:trace-B} and \eqref{eq:normal-quadratic} imply
\[
  \tr(P_{L_\ell}B)=0.
\]

The direction space of \(\Delta\) decomposes as
\[
  U_\Delta=L_\ell\oplus R_\ell
\]
for a three-dimensional subspace
\(R_\ell\subseteq L_\ell^\perp\).  Moreover,
\[
  L_\ell^\perp=\operatorname{span}(n_\ell)\oplus L^\perp.
\]
For \(w=\lambda n_\ell+r\), with \(r\in L^\perp\), the facts
\(Br=0\) and \eqref{eq:normal-quadratic} give
\(w^{\mathsf T}Bw=0\).  Because \(B\) is symmetric, polarization
shows that its compression to \(L_\ell^\perp\) is zero.  Hence
\(\tr(P_{R_\ell}B)=0\), and altogether
\begin{equation}
  \tr(P_{U_\Delta}B)=0.
  \label{eq:triangle-trace}
\end{equation}

Equations \eqref{eq:edge-trace} and \eqref{eq:triangle-trace} cover
the two families in \eqref{eq:hyperedge-families}, hence all \(44\)
hyperedges.  By \cref{lem:trace}, the velocity \(u\) in
\eqref{eq:flex} lies in \(\ker J_{\cS}(p)\).

\subsection{Nontriviality and the rank bound}

Suppose, for contradiction, that \(u\) is Euclidean-trivial.  Then
there are a skew-symmetric matrix \(A\) and a vector \(t\) such that
\[
  B(p(v)-p(z_0))=Ap(v)+t
\]
for all ten vertices.  Substitution of \(v=z_0\) gives
\(t=-Ap(z_0)\), so
\[
  B(p(v)-p(z_0))=A(p(v)-p(z_0))
\]
for every vertex \(v\).  A generic ten-point placement affinely spans
\(\R^7\); its differences from \(p(z_0)\) therefore span \(\R^7\),
forcing \(B=A\).  This is impossible because \(B\) is nonzero and
symmetric while \(A\) is skew-symmetric.

Thus \(\ker J_{\cS}(p)\) contains the \(28\)-dimensional trivial
motion space and the independent motion \(u\).  Rank--nullity yields
\[
  \rank J_{\cS}(p)
  \leq 70-(28+1)=41,
\]
which proves \cref{thm:main}.

\section{Exact cross-checks and verification boundary}
\label{sec:verification}

The proof of \cref{thm:main} is the symbolic argument in the preceding
section.  The following finite computations do not carry the generic
conclusion.

An exhaustive enumeration considered all
\(\binom{20}{8}=125970\) choices of eight triangles on six labelled
vertices.  A topology filter retained \(195\) labelled sphere face
tables.  Quotienting by the \(S_6\)-action gave the two types in
\cref{sec:types}, with labelled orbit sizes \(180\) and \(15\), and
automorphism-group orders \(4\) and \(48\), respectively.  An
independent graph-atlas reconstruction found the same two planar,
three-connected, six-vertex triangulations.

At one exact integer placement (deterministic seed \(260830\)), each
type passed the following checks: the ten points had affine rank seven;
all \(44\) Gram determinants were nonzero; the \(28\) standard trivial
motions and the motion in \eqref{eq:flex} lay in the exact kernel; and
adding the displayed motion raised the motion-space rank from \(28\)
to \(29\).  The specialized Jacobian had rank \(40\) modulo the prime
\(2147483647\).  Exact \(40\times40\) minors were nonzero, with \(394\)
and \(395\) decimal digits for the stacked and octahedral types.  These
specialized calculations are cross-checks and lower-rank witnesses at
the chosen coordinates; they are not the source of the generic upper
bound.

A separate checker reconstructed the two sphere types from the graph
atlas, rebuilt all \(44\) rows using Cayley--Menger determinant
derivatives, verified the identities in
\eqref{eq:two-identities}--\eqref{eq:facet-identities}, and recomputed
the exact minors with NetworkX~3.5 and SymPy~1.14.0.  It reported
\texttt{PASS}.  That checker reused the stored face tables, coordinates,
and candidate minor indices, so it was not completely input-isolated;
its independent row construction and symbolic checks nevertheless
provide a noncircular cross-check.  The generic claim remains the
symbolic proof above.

The mathematical argument received an independent verdict of
\texttt{CORRECT}.  A separate paper-level review checked the theorem
quantifiers, the expanded determinant calculation in \cref{lem:trace},
the source interfaces, and the complete hyperedge tables, and likewise
reported \texttt{CORRECT}.

\section{Relation to the source paper and scope}

The source paper proves volume-rigidity results for lower skeletons and
for the codimension-two manifold case in dimensions \(4,5,6\), and it
states Conjecture~22 as a proposed family of base cases for extending
the method \cite{CruickshankJacksonTanigawa2026}.  The theorem here
addresses only the next numerical slice, \(d=7\), and only the
simplicial-cone hypergraphs appearing in that conjecture.  It does not
settle the source paper's broader Conjecture~2, since that extension
requires further arguments even if all the proposed base cases were
available.

This note makes no claim of public priority, of being the first
counterexample, or of establishing the current public status of the
all-dimensional conjecture.  Public novelty or priority is
\texttt{NOT\_ESTABLISHED}.
